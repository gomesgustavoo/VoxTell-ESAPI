    How it works typical structure set: ~13 s

    Four steps, and you never leave Eclipse.

    VoxTell installs as an ESAPI script. It reads the image the planner already has open, and it puts the result back where the planner already works.

    1. Step 01

      Open the plan

      Launch VoxTell from the Eclipse tools menu. It picks up the active image series and its geometry directly — no manual export, no file picker, no filename to get wrong.

    2. Step 02

      Name the structures

      Type up to 16 structures per job, in your own words. The series uploads once; every further prompt on the same image moves no image data at all.

    3. Step 03

      The server segments

      One GPU job per request on a fair-share queue, with your position and estimated wait shown live. Uploads are deleted automatically; results expire in 24 hours.

    4. Step 04

      Straight into your TPS

      Contours are written into the open plan's structure set through ESAPI, ready to review, edit and approve. Nothing to import, and nothing leaves Eclipse for you to reconcile later.

    Safety & data results expire in 24 h

    It cannot commit anything to a patient on its own.

    This tool proposes contours in a plan that a human being will treat from. The limits below are structural, not policy.

    • The plugin never saves.

      It opens a modification session and writes structures, and that is all. The planner saves in Eclipse, or discards. There is no code path by which VoxTell commits a change to a patient without a deliberate human action.

    • Always review before clinical use.

      Segmentation output is for research and planning support — always review contours before clinical use.

    • Not a medical device.

      No regulatory clearance is claimed or implied. You remain responsible for the clinical acceptability of every structure.

    • Model provenance is public.

      VoxTell model by MIC, DKFZ Heidelberg (arXiv:2511.11450), accepted at CVPR 2026. This service is an independent extension of it.

    • No DICOM headers leave the workstation.

      What is uploaded is a gzipped int16 voxel array plus a geometry object — spacing, origin and orientation. No patient name, no ID, no dates, no institution.

    • Uploads are deleted on a clock.

      A series is dropped 120 minutes after its last use and 8 hours after upload, whichever comes first. Results expire 24 hours after the job finishes, and the objects are deleted.

    • Per-account isolation, and deletion on demand.

      Every query is scoped to your account. You can list everything held for you and delete any of it immediately, from the dashboard or the API.

    • Nothing is used for training.

      Your images are not retained for model development, ever.

    Contours are a starting point. Auto-segmentation shortens the mechanical part of contouring; it does not transfer clinical judgement. Review every structure on every slice that matters to the dose.

    Requirements ESAPI · RTM 16.1 · .NET 4.6.2

    What your workstation needs.

    • Varian Eclipse with ESAPI scripting.

      Built against the RTM 16.1 assemblies and .NET Framework 4.6.2, on Windows. The script must be approved for write access, since writing structures is the whole point.

    • Outbound HTTPS to two hostnames.

      voxtell.dicomsegvr.com for the API and s3.dicomsegvr.com for the bulk upload. A firewall that allows only the first will sign in successfully and then hang at upload — which is the single most common installation failure.

    • An account, and a key per workstation.

      Sign in from the plugin, or paste a workstation key minted in the dashboard. Keys are shown once and can be revoked individually, so a decommissioned workstation does not take the rest of the clinic with it.

    • No server-side install.

      Nothing runs in your data centre. Two DLLs go into your published-scripts share and the GPU work happens here.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does this replace manual contouring?

    No, and it is not sold as though it does. It removes the mechanical part — outlining organs you have outlined a thousand times — so the time you spend is spent on judgement: the target, the boundaries that matter to the dose, and the slices where the automatic result is wrong. Every structure arrives unapproved and editable.

    What exactly leaves the workstation?

    A gzipped int16 voxel array and a geometry object holding spacing, origin and orientation. No DICOM headers, so no patient name, ID, dates or institution. The series is deleted 120 minutes after its last use, or 8 hours after upload, whichever comes first.

    How is a job counted against my quota?

    One submission of one image series is one job, whether it asks for one structure or sixteen. Quota counts submissions rather than completions, so a cancelled job is not refunded — that is what stops the queue being farmed. Extra prompts on a series you have already uploaded still count as a job, but move no image data.

    What happens when the trial ends?

    Nothing is deleted and nothing is charged. Segmentation stops until you pick a plan, and your keys and history stay exactly as they were. There is no card on file to fail.

    Why is DicomSegVR included?

    They are the same company and the same account, and the two halves are useful together: VoxTell writes structures into the plan, DicomSegVR lets you inspect the result in 3D and VR before you approve it. One subscription covers both at the matching tier, so there is nothing extra to buy or to reconcile.

    Can several workstations share one account?

    Yes — that is what workstation keys are for. Mint one per machine so you can revoke a single workstation without touching the others. Your plan sets how many keys and how many simultaneous segmentations you get; the monthly quota is shared across all of them.

    Does it work on MR and PET, or only CT?

    All three. The model was trained on more than 62,000 CT, MR and PET volumes, and the prompt is text rather than a modality-specific label. CT is the common case in planning; MR and PET work through the same path with no extra setup.

    What if my structure is not in the bundled bank?

    It still runs. The bank of 14,194 phrasings is a cache of pre-computed text embeddings, not a list of permitted structures — an unlisted phrase is encoded on the spot. VoxTell is state-of-the-art zero-shot on unseen datasets and generalises to related unseen classes, so an unusual phrasing is a latency cost, not a refusal.
