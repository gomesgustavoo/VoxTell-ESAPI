In Eclipse five steps

You never leave the planning system.

The plugin reads the open image, uploads it once, and puts the contours back into the active structure set. There is nothing to export and nothing to re-import.

  1. 01

    Install once

    Two DLLs into the Eclipse scripts folder, approved for write access. No server, no CUDA, no GPU, and no inbound firewall rule.

  2. 02

    Sign in once

    The plugin opens your browser against the same account as the dashboard. On a workstation with no free port or no registered browser it falls back to a short code you approve from your phone. Remembered between runs.

  3. 03

    Name the structures

    One per line, up to 16 in a job. r. 11th rib, hepatic parenchyma, the FDG-avid nodule — there is no list to match and no vocabulary to learn.

  4. 04

    Review before anything is written

    Every prompt comes back as a row: the structure Id it would create, its DICOM type, the voxel and contour counts, and whether it is new or overwrites an existing structure. Rename it, retype it, or untick it.

  5. 05

    Import, then save

    Only ticked rows are written, and re-running a prompt replaces its contours rather than superimposing them. The plugin never saves for you — you save in Eclipse.

Your data results expire in 24 h

Nothing that identifies a patient leaves the workstation.

Not because a de-identification step strips it, but because the wire format has nowhere to put it.

  • The upload has no field for an identifier. What goes out is the image geometry, the voxels as a gzipped int16 array, and your prompt text. No DICOM headers, no names, no record numbers, no UIDs — the plugin never reads them.
  • Everything is deleted on a clock. Contours expire 24 hours after a job finishes. A held series expires after 120 minutes idle and 8 hours absolutely. Nothing here is an archive.
  • Per-account isolation, and deletion on demand. Jobs and series are scoped to your account, and deleting one purges its objects immediately rather than waiting for the clock.
  • Nothing is used for training. Your images do not become anyone's dataset, ours included.
Requirements ESAPI · RTM 16.1 · .NET 4.6.2

What the workstation needs.

  • Eclipse with ESAPI scripting enabled. RTM 16.1 and .NET Framework 4.6.2, and the script approved for write access by whoever administers scripting at your site.
  • Outbound HTTPS to two hostnames. voxtell.dicomsegvr.com and s3.dicomsegvr.com. No inbound rule, no VPN, no port forward.
  • A structure set that already exists on the open patient. The plugin imports into the active set; it cannot create one from scratch.
  • Nothing on your servers. No GPU, no Linux host, no CUDA, no model files, nothing to patch. That is the whole reason this service exists.
The engine 14,194 phrasings · 7,137 with laterality

Named, cited, and replaceable.

Segmentation runs on VoxTell v1.1 from MIC at DKFZ Heidelberg — state-of-the-art zero-shot on unseen datasets, accepted at CVPR 2026 (Rokuss et al., arXiv:2511.11450). It ships with a bank of 14,194 phrasings, so a familiar prompt never touches the text encoder. Anything outside the bank is attempted zero-shot rather than refused.

14,194 phrasings

    Every result names the model that produced it, and the wire contract is contours in DICOM patient coordinates rather than anything model-specific — so the engine can be replaced the day something measurably better ships, without a plugin release. What you pay for is GPU time, hosted storage and support. The model weights are third-party research artefacts, credited in full at the foot of this page.

    What this does not do.

    • It is not a medical device. No regulatory clearance is claimed, for any use, in any jurisdiction.
    • Contours are a starting point. Auto-segmentation shortens the mechanical part of contouring. It does not transfer clinical judgement, and every structure needs review before a plan is built on it.
    • Results are not bit-reproducible. cuDNN autotuning is on, so re-running the same series moves a handful of boundary voxels — 0.001% to 0.009% — and can change a contour count by a fragment.
    • Eclipse only, today. The ESAPI plugin is the only planning-system integration that exists, and there is no DICOM RTSTRUCT export yet — so RayStation, Monaco and Pinnacle are not supported. Tell us if that is what you need; it changes what we build next.
    • One job is one image series. Up to 16 prompts, 512 × 512 × 1024 voxels, 1 GiB compressed. Anything larger is a conversation rather than a setting.
    • The GPU is shared. One segmentation runs at a time below Enterprise, first-come, with long-waiting jobs aging ahead of new ones. A busy minute is a wait, not a failure.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does this replace manual contouring?

    No. It removes the mechanical part — tracing an organ slice by slice — and hands you a structure set to correct. The review step exists because that correction is the job, and because a planner is accountable for what ends up in the plan.

    How is a job counted against my quota?

    One job is one submission of one series, whether it carries one prompt or sixteen, counted at submission rather than at completion. A failed upload costs nothing; cancelling a running job still counts, because the GPU time was spent. Quotas run on the UTC calendar month.

    Can several workstations share one account?

    Yes — that is what workstation keys are for. Mint one per machine from the dashboard, with an expiry, and revoke it on its own when a workstation is decommissioned. One account, one quota, and one job list covering all of them.

    What happens when the trial ends?

    Nothing is charged and nothing is removed from your workstation. The plugin stays installed and signed in; new jobs are refused until you pick a plan. No card is on file during the trial, so there is nothing to cancel.
